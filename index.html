UNIT I

Stopping by Woods • A Dog's Tale

1. Poem – Stopping by Woods on a Snowy Evening

Poet: Robert FrostTheme: Nature and responsibility

Understand the poem like a story

A man travels on a cold, snowy evening. He sees a beautiful forest covered with snow and stops to enjoy it. His horse feels confused because they have stopped in a lonely place. The horse shakes its bells as if asking why they have stopped. The poet enjoys the quiet forest, but remembers that he has promises and duties to complete. So he continues his journey.

മഞ്ഞ് മൂടിയ മനോഹരമായ കാട് കണ്ട യാത്രക്കാരൻ അവിടെ നിൽക്കുന്നു. പക്ഷേ ചെയ്യാനുള്ള ഉത്തരവാദിത്തങ്ങൾ ഓർത്ത് വീണ്ടും യാത്ര തുടരുന്നു.

Stanza-by-stanza meaning

  1. Stanza 1: The poet stops near a forest while snow is falling. The owner lives in the village.
  2. Stanza 2: The horse thinks stopping there is strange. There is no farmhouse nearby.
  3. Stanza 3: The horse shakes its bells. The only other sounds are the wind and falling snow.
  4. Stanza 4: The woods are beautiful, but the poet has promises to keep and miles to travel.

Important points

  • Woods = beauty, peace and mystery.
  • Horse = reminder of duty.
  • Sleep = rest; it can also suggest death.
  • Repeated line: “And miles to go before I sleep.”
  • Poetic devices: imagery, personification and repetition.

5-mark answer: Explain the theme of responsibility.

Robert Frost's poem Stopping by Woods on a Snowy Evening explains the importance of responsibility. The poet stops near a beautiful forest on a snowy evening. He enjoys watching the snow falling on the trees. His horse feels confused and shakes its bells. This reminds the poet that he must continue his journey. The poet wants to enjoy nature, but he remembers his promises and duties. The line “And miles to go before I sleep” shows that he has many responsibilities. The poem teaches us to complete our duties before taking rest.

For an 8-mark answer, add these points

  1. Introduction about Robert Frost.
  2. Explain the snowy forest.
  3. Explain the horse's reaction.
  4. Describe the silence of nature.
  5. Explain the meaning of sleep.
  6. Explain the repeated last line.
  7. Write the theme of responsibility.
  8. Conclude that we must not forget our duties.

Memory trick: Beautiful forest → Horse → Bells → Promises → Duty.

2. Short story – A Dog's Tale

Author: Mark TwainTheme: Loyalty and animal cruelty

Easy story summary

The story is narrated by a dog. The dog loves its family and is especially protective of a baby. When the baby is in danger because of a fire, the dog bravely saves the baby. But the master misunderstands the dog's good action and beats it. The dog's puppy is also used in a medical experiment. The dog suffers, but continues to show love and loyalty.

നായ തന്റെ കുടുംബത്തെയും കുഞ്ഞിനെയും സ്നേഹിക്കുന്നു. കുഞ്ഞിനെ രക്ഷിച്ചിട്ടും മനുഷ്യർ നായയെ തെറ്റിദ്ധരിച്ച് ക്രൂരമായി പെരുമാറുന്നു.

Important points

  • Narrator: a dog; breed: collie.
  • The dog saves the baby.
  • The master misunderstands and beats the dog.
  • The puppy is used in a medical experiment.
  • Theme: loyalty, sacrifice, animal cruelty and kindness.

5-mark answer: How does the story show human cruelty?

Mark Twain's A Dog's Tale is a sad story about a loyal dog. The dog loves its family and protects the baby. When the baby is in danger, the dog saves its life. This shows its courage and loyalty. But the master does not understand the dog's good action and beats it. The dog's puppy is also used in a medical experiment. Through this story, Mark Twain shows that humans sometimes treat animals cruelly even when animals show love and loyalty. We should love animals and never hurt them.

8-mark points

  1. Introduction to Mark Twain.
  2. Story is narrated by a dog.
  3. Dog's love for family.
  4. Dog saves the baby.
  5. Master's cruel behaviour.
  6. Medical experiment on puppy.
  7. Dog's suffering and loyalty.
  8. Message: respect and protect animals.

Memory trick: Loyalty → Baby saved → Cruel master → Animal rights.

UNIT II

Love After Love • The New Dress

3. Poem – Love After Love

Poet: Derek WalcottTheme: Self-love and acceptance

Understand the poem

Imagine you loved someone very much, but that person leaves you. You become sad and forget to love yourself. The poet says that one day you will meet your own self again. You will look into the mirror and smile. You will welcome yourself like a guest, give your heart back to yourself and forget painful memories. The poem teaches us to love and accept ourselves.

മറ്റുള്ളവരെ സ്നേഹിക്കുന്നതിനൊപ്പം നമ്മളെയും സ്നേഹിക്കണം. പഴയ വേദനകൾ മറന്ന് സ്വന്തം ജീവിതത്തെ സന്തോഷത്തോടെ സ്വീകരിക്കണം.

Stanza meaning

  1. You will meet yourself again and welcome yourself happily.
  2. You will give your heart, care and attention back to yourself.
  3. Forget painful memories and old love letters. Accept your identity.

Symbols and key points

  • Mirror = self-recognition.
  • Stranger = your forgotten self.
  • Wine and bread = hospitality and care.
  • Love letters = past memories.
  • Tone = gentle and hopeful.

5-mark answer: Importance of self-love

Derek Walcott's poem Love After Love explains the importance of loving ourselves. After facing sadness and heartbreak, we must learn to accept ourselves again. One day, we will meet our own self like a stranger and welcome it happily. The poet asks us to give our heart back to ourselves. He tells us to forget painful memories and old love letters. The mirror represents self-recognition. The poem encourages us to respect our identity and enjoy our own lives. Self-love is important for happiness and peace.

8-mark points

  1. Introduction to Derek Walcott.
  2. Meaning of self-love.
  3. Meeting ourselves in the mirror.
  4. The forgotten self.
  5. Giving our heart back to ourselves.
  6. Removing old memories.
  7. Symbols: mirror, food and love letters.
  8. Conclusion: love and accept yourself.

Memory trick: Mirror → Meet yourself → Love yourself → Forget past → Enjoy life.

4. Short story – The New Dress

Author: Virginia WoolfMain character: Mabel WaringTheme: Insecurity and social pressure

Easy story summary

Mabel Waring attends a party at Mrs. Dalloway's house wearing a new yellow-green dress. She looks into the mirror and feels that her dress is ugly and old-fashioned. She thinks everyone is laughing at her. Rose Shaw says the dress looks charming, but Mabel thinks she is not sincere. Mabel compares herself with fashionable women and loses confidence. She remembers feeling happy when the dressmaker made the dress, but at the party she feels unhappy and decides to leave.

മറ്റുള്ളവർ എന്ത് ചിന്തിക്കും എന്ന് അമിതമായി പേടിക്കുമ്പോൾ നമ്മുടെ ആത്മവിശ്വാസം നഷ്ടപ്പെടും.

Characters and themes

  • Mabel Waring = insecure woman.
  • Rose Shaw = fashionable woman.
  • Mrs. Dalloway = party host.
  • Miss Milan = dressmaker.
  • Themes: lack of confidence, class differences, jealousy, fashion and social pressure.
  • Green dress = symbol of Mabel's insecurity.

5-mark answer: Social insecurity in The New Dress

Virginia Woolf's The New Dress is a story about a woman named Mabel Waring. Mabel attends a party wearing a new dress. When she looks in the mirror, she feels that the dress is ugly. She thinks everyone is judging her appearance. Although Rose Shaw says the dress is charming, Mabel does not believe her. She compares herself with other fashionable women and feels inferior. Her dress becomes a symbol of her insecurity and lack of confidence. The story teaches us not to judge ourselves by other people's opinions.

8-mark points

  1. Introduction to Virginia Woolf.
  2. Mabel attends Mrs. Dalloway's party.
  3. Her new dress makes her uncomfortable.
  4. She compares herself with others.
  5. Rose Shaw praises the dress.
  6. Mabel thinks others judge her.
  7. Dress symbolises insecurity.
  8. Self-confidence is more important than fashion.

Memory trick: Mabel → Party → Dress → Mirror → Insecurity → Leaves party.

UNIT III

The Solitary Reaper • The Doll's House

5. Poem – The Solitary Reaper

Poet: William WordsworthTheme: Nature and music

Easy story explanation

The poet walks through a beautiful field in Scotland. He sees a young girl working alone, cutting and collecting wheat while singing a beautiful song. Her voice fills the valley. The poet compares her song with the nightingale and cuckoo. He does not understand the language of her song, but wonders whether it is about sadness, old battles or everyday life. He walks away, but the music remains in his heart.

വയലിൽ ജോലി ചെയ്യുമ്പോൾ പാടുന്ന പെൺകുട്ടിയുടെ ശബ്ദം കവിയെ സ്വാധീനിക്കുന്നു. പാട്ട് അവസാനിച്ചാലും അതിന്റെ ഓർമ്മ മനസ്സിൽ നിലനിൽക്കുന്നു.

Stanza meaning

  1. A lonely girl harvests grain and sings in a field.
  2. Her voice is sweeter than the nightingale and cuckoo.
  3. The poet wonders what her song is about.
  4. The poet leaves, but remembers the song.

Important points

  • Girl = Highland girl; place = Scottish Highlands.
  • Work = harvesting grain.
  • Birds = nightingale and cuckoo.
  • Poetic devices = simile, imagery and alliteration.
  • Theme = nature, music and human emotion.

5-mark answer: Beauty of the reaper's song

William Wordsworth's poem The Solitary Reaper describes a young girl singing while working in a field. The girl works alone and cuts the grain. Her beautiful voice fills the entire valley. The poet compares her song with the songs of the nightingale and cuckoo. He does not understand the meaning of her song, but feels its deep emotion. The poet listens silently and continues his journey. Even after leaving the place, the music remains in his heart. The poem shows the power of music and the beauty of nature.

8-mark points

  1. Introduction to Wordsworth.
  2. The lonely Highland girl.
  3. Her work in the field.
  4. Sweetness of her song.
  5. Comparison with birds.
  6. Mystery of the song.
  7. Music remains in the poet's heart.
  8. Ordinary moments create beautiful memories.

Memory trick: Girl → Field → Song → Birds → Mystery → Memory.

The Burnell sisters Isabel, Lottie and Kezia receive a beautiful doll's house. It has tiny rooms, furniture and a little lamp. Kezia likes the lamp most. At school there are two poor sisters, Lil and Else Kelvey. Other children avoid them because their family is poor. Kezia is kind and invites them to see the doll's house. Aunt Beryl sees them and angrily sends them away. Later Else remembers the little lamp and smiles.

പാവപ്പെട്ട കുട്ടികളെ മറ്റുള്ളവർ ഒഴിവാക്കുന്നു. എന്നാൽ Kezia അവരോട് കരുണ കാണിക്കുന്നു. ചെറിയ വിളക്ക് പ്രതീക്ഷയുടെയും നന്മയുടെയും അടയാളമാണ്.

Characters

Symbol of the little lamp

The lamp represents hope, kindness, equality and light in a dark world.

5-mark answer: Importance of the little lamp

Katherine Mansfield's The Doll's House is a story about social discrimination and kindness. The Burnell sisters receive a beautiful doll's house. Kezia likes the little lamp more than anything else. She invites the poor Kelvey sisters to see the doll's house. Aunt Beryl becomes angry and sends them away. However, Else remembers the little lamp and smiles. The lamp represents hope, kindness and equality. The story teaches us to treat everyone equally, regardless of social class.

On His Blindness • Everyday Use

7. Poem – On His Blindness

Poet: John MiltonForm: Petrarchan sonnetTheme: Faith and patience

Easy explanation

John Milton is a talented writer who loses his eyesight. He becomes sad because he cannot use his writing talent as before. He worries that he cannot serve God. Then a voice called Patience answers him. Patience explains that God does not need human work. People who accept difficulties with faith and patience also serve God. The famous final line is “They also serve who only stand and wait.”

കാഴ്ച നഷ്ടപ്പെട്ടതിൽ മിൽട്ടൺ വിഷമിക്കുന്നു. പക്ഷേ ക്ഷമയും ദൈവവിശ്വാസവും ഉള്ളവരും ദൈവത്തെ സേവിക്കുന്നവരാണ് എന്ന് കവിത പറയുന്നു.

Important points

  • Problem = blindness.
  • Talent = writing.
  • Patience = inner wisdom.
  • Sonnet = 14 lines.
  • Famous line = “They also serve who only stand and wait.”

5-mark answer: Importance of patience

John Milton's poem On His Blindness explains the importance of patience and faith in God. Milton loses his eyesight and becomes sad. He worries that he cannot use his writing talent to serve God. He asks whether God will punish him for not using his talent. Then Patience answers that God does not need human work or gifts. People who accept their difficulties with faith also serve God. The famous line “They also serve who only stand and wait” explains that patience is also a form of service. We must face difficulties with patience and faith.

8-mark points

  1. Introduction to John Milton.
  2. Milton loses his eyesight.
  3. His sadness and worries.
  4. Writing talent becomes difficult to use.
  5. His question to God.
  6. Patience gives an answer.
  7. Meaning of the famous final line.
  8. Faith and patience are important.

Memory trick: Blindness → Sadness → Question → Patience → Faith → Peace.

  • Hakim-a-barber = Dee's partner.

Symbol of the quilts

The quilts represent family history, love, tradition, cultural heritage and connection between generations.

Alice Walker's Everyday Use is a story about family heritage and traditions. The story has three important characters: Mama, Dee and Maggie. Mama decides to give the quilts to Maggie because she respects family traditions. The quilts symbolise family history, love and cultural heritage. UNIT V

The Chimney Sweeper • Nitya

9. Poem – The Chimney Sweeper

Poet: William BlakeTheme: Child labour and hope

Easy story explanation

A small boy loses his mother. His father sells him to work as a chimney sweeper. The boy works in dangerous conditions and sleeps in soot. His friend Tom Dacre cries when his curly hair is shaved. The speaker comforts Tom. That night Tom dreams that thousands of chimney sweepers are locked inside black coffins. An angel comes with a bright key and frees them. The children run through green fields and enjoy the sunlight. Tom wakes up happy, but the children must return to work.

ചെറിയ കുട്ടികളെ അപകടകരമായ ജോലിക്ക് നിർബന്ധിക്കുന്നതിനെ വില്യം ബ്ലേക്ക് വിമർശിക്കുന്നു. കുട്ടികൾക്ക് സുരക്ഷയും വിദ്യാഭ്യാസവും സന്തോഷവും ലഭിക്കണം.

Symbols and points

  • Tom Dacre = the boy's friend.
  • Black coffins = suffering and death.
  • Angel = hope and freedom.
  • Bright key = freedom.
  • Theme = child labour and exploitation.

5-mark answer: Child labour

William Blake's poem The Chimney Sweeper explains the suffering of poor children. The poem begins with a small boy whose mother has died. His father sells him to work as a chimney sweeper. The children work in dangerous conditions and live a painful life. The boy's friend Tom Dacre has a dream in which an angel frees thousands of children from black coffins. The dream represents freedom, happiness and hope. Blake criticises child labour and the cruelty of society. Every child deserves love, education and a happy childhood.

8-mark points

  1. Introduction to William Blake.
  2. The boy loses his mother.
  3. Father sells him for work.
  4. Tom Dacre cries after his hair is shaved.
  5. Tom's dream about black coffins.
  6. Angel opens the coffins.
  7. Children enjoy freedom in green fields.
  8. Society must protect children from exploitation.

Memory trick: Mother dies → Father sells → Tom cries → Angel → Freedom → Child labour.

10. Short story – Nitya

Author: R. K. NarayanTheme: Tradition versus modernity

Easy story summary

Nitya is a young college student. When he was a small child, he suffered from a serious illness. His worried parents prayed to God and promised that if he recovered, they would shave his head and offer his hair at a temple. Nitya recovered, but the promise was not fulfilled for many years. Now he is twenty years old. His parents decide to take him to the temple. Nitya refuses because he does not believe in the ritual and wants to make his own decisions. His parents believe the promise must be fulfilled. At the temple he continues to refuse. Finally, his parents cannot force him and leave disappointed.

മാതാപിതാക്കൾ പഴയ ആചാരങ്ങളിൽ വിശ്വസിക്കുന്നു. എന്നാൽ നിത്യ സ്വന്തം തീരുമാനങ്ങൾ എടുക്കാൻ ആഗ്രഹിക്കുന്ന ആധുനിക യുവാവാണ്. ഇത് തലമുറകൾ തമ്മിലുള്ള അഭിപ്രായ വ്യത്യാസം കാണിക്കുന്നു.

Main characters

  • Nitya = modern, independent young man.
  • Father = traditional and religious.
  • Mother = devoted and emotional.
  • Temple priest = represents religious tradition.

Main themes

  • Tradition versus modernity.
  • Generation gap.
  • Faith and freedom.
  • Individual choice.
  • Parent-child relationship.

5-mark answer: Tradition and modernity

R. K. Narayan's Nitya is a story about the conflict between old traditions and modern thinking. When Nitya was a small child, he suffered from a serious illness. His parents promised God that they would offer his hair at a temple if he recovered. After many years, his parents decide to fulfil the promise. But Nitya is now twenty years old. He refuses to shave his head because he believes that he should make his own decisions. His parents believe strongly in religion and tradition. Nitya believes in freedom and logical thinking. Finally, his parents cannot force him and leave the temple disappointed. The story teaches us to respect and understand each other's beliefs.

8-mark points

  1. Introduction to R. K. Narayan.
  2. Nitya's childhood illness.
  3. Parents make a religious promise.
  4. Nitya grows into a young man.
  5. Parents decide to fulfil the vow.
  6. Nitya refuses to shave his head.
  7. Conflict at the temple.
  8. Faith, freedom and understanding are important.

Memory trick: Illness → Promise → Twenty years → Temple → Refusal → Disappointment.

Grammar – Easy rules

Learn the rule and one example.

1. Articles – A, An, The
  • A: before a consonant sound – a boy, a dog, a university.
  • An: before a vowel sound – an apple, an umbrella, an honest man.
  • The: for a particular or unique thing – the Sun, the Moon, the Earth.

Easy trick: A = consonant sound; AN = vowel sound; THE = particular thing.

7. Yes / No questions

These questions can be answered with YES or NO.

Examples: Are you a student? – Yes, I am. Do you like coffee? – Yes, I do. Can you swim? – No, I cannot.

StatementQuestion
She speaks English.Does she speak English?
They watched a movie.Did they watch a movie?
He can play.Can he play?
The teacher is here.Is the teacher here?
You like ice cream.Do you like ice cream?
She will come.Will she come?

Important: After do, does and did, use the base form of the verb. Correct: Does she like tea? Not: Does she likes tea?
