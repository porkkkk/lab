POEM 01

The Pulley

George Herbert
Poetry

Simple Summary

The poem explains God's love for human beings. When God created humans, He gave them many gifts such as strength, beauty, wisdom, honour and pleasure. But God did not give them complete rest.

God knew that if human beings had everything, they might forget Him. So He kept rest away from them. When people become tired and unhappy, they may turn towards God for peace and comfort.

Important Points

  • God created human beings with love.
  • He gave them many blessings and gifts.
  • He kept rest as the one gift that was not given completely.
  • Human restlessness can make people remember God.
  • The poem explains the relationship between God and human beings.

Main Themes

  • God's love and wisdom.
  • Human nature and desire.
  • Faith and spiritual connection.
  • The search for peace and rest.
Remember: God gives humans many blessings but keeps rest away so that they will turn towards Him.
POEM 02

Sonnet 30

William Shakespeare
Sonnet

Simple Summary

In Sonnet 30, Shakespeare remembers his past problems and losses. When he thinks about the past, he feels sad. He remembers old friends and things that he has lost.

He feels pain because of these memories. But at the end, he remembers a dear friend. This memory brings happiness and makes him feel that his losses are less painful.

Important Points

  • The poet thinks about his past.
  • He remembers lost friends and old sadness.
  • He feels sorrow for things he cannot get back.
  • The memory of a dear friend gives him comfort.
  • Love and friendship help him overcome sadness.

Poetic Form

  • It is a Shakespearean sonnet.
  • It has 14 lines.
  • It follows the rhyme scheme ABAB CDCD EFEF GG.
  • The final two lines are called the couplet.
Remember: Memories of a true friend can bring happiness even during sadness.

Thank You, M'am

Langston Hughes
Short Story

LESSON 06

The Last Leaf

O. Henry
Short Story

Simple Summary

The story is about two young artists, Sue and Johnsy. Johnsy becomes seriously ill. She loses hope and believes that she will die when the last leaf on the ivy vine outside her window falls.

Sue tries to encourage her. Their old neighbour Behrman hears about Johnsy's belief. During a stormy night, he paints a leaf on the wall so that it looks real.

The leaf does not fall. Johnsy sees it and gets hope to live. Behrman catches pneumonia and dies. His painted leaf becomes his masterpiece.

Main Characters

  • Johnsy: A sick young artist who loses hope.
  • Sue: Johnsy's caring friend.
  • Behrman: An old artist who sacrifices his life to save Johnsy.

Important Points

  • Johnsy becomes ill and loses hope.
  • She connects her life with the falling ivy leaves.
  • Sue tries to give her courage.
  • Behrman paints a leaf during a storm.
  • Johnsy regains hope after seeing the leaf.
  • Behrman dies after his sacrifice.

Themes

  • Hope and courage.
  • Friendship and love.
  • Sacrifice and kindness.
  • The power of art.
Remember: Behrman's painted leaf gives Johnsy hope and saves her life.

Rabindranath Tagore
Short Story

Simple Summary

The story is about Raicharan, a loyal servant who takes care of Anukul's young son. One day, while Raicharan is looking after the child near the Padma River, the baby disappears.

Raicharan feels guilty and heartbroken. Later, he has a son of his own named Phailna. He brings up the boy with great care and gives him everything he can.

Raicharan later gives Phailna to Anukul, believing that the child should receive the life and position that belonged to Anukul's missing son. The story explores loyalty, guilt and sacrifice.

Important Points

  • The baby disappears near the Padma River.
  • Raicharan feels deep guilt.
  • He later raises his own son, Phailna.
  • He gives Phailna to Anukul.
  • The story shows the depth of Raicharan's sacrifice.

Themes

Remember: Raicharan's actions show his loyalty, guilt and deep sacrifice.
LESSON 08

The Open Window

Saki (H. H. Munro)
Short Story

Simple Summary

The story is about Framton Nuttel, who visits a house to improve his health. He meets a young girl named Vera. She tells him a frightening story about an open window.

Vera says that her aunt's husband and brothers went hunting and never returned. She claims that her aunt keeps the window open because she hopes they will come back.

When the men return from hunting, Framton believes they are ghosts. He becomes frightened and runs away. Vera then creates another explanation for his sudden escape.

Main Characters

  • Vera: A clever young girl who tells imaginary stories.
  • Framton Nuttel: A nervous visitor who believes Vera.
  • Mrs. Sappleton: Vera's aunt.

Important Points

  • Framton visits Mrs. Sappleton's house.
  • Vera tells him a false story about the open window.
  • Framton believes the story.
  • The hunting party returns home.
  • Framton thinks they are ghosts and runs away.
  • Vera makes up another story to explain his escape.

Themes

  • Imagination and deception.
  • Fear and misunderstanding.
  • Humour and surprise.
Remember: Vera's imagination and clever lies create the surprise ending.

GRAMMAR 01

Clauses and Sentences

Grammar

What is a Sentence?

A sentence is a group of words that gives a complete meaning.

Example: She is reading a book.

What is a Clause?

A clause is a group of words that contains a subject and a verb.

Example: When I came home, my mother was cooking.

Types of Clauses

  • Main / Independent Clause: Gives complete meaning. Example: I went to school.
  • Subordinate / Dependent Clause: Does not give complete meaning alone. Example: Because I was sick.
  • Noun Clause: Works like a noun. Example: I know what he wants.
  • Adjective Clause: Describes a noun. Example: The boy who won is my friend.
  • Adverb Clause: Tells when, why, where or how. Example: I stayed home because it rained.

Types of Sentences

  • Simple: One independent clause. Example: She sings.
  • Compound: Two independent clauses joined together. Example: I came and he left.
  • Complex: One main clause and one dependent clause. Example: I slept because I was tired.

Common Joining Words

FANBOYS: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So.

GRAMMAR 02

Subject–Verb Agreement (Concord)

Grammar

Meaning

The subject and verb must agree in number. A singular subject normally takes a singular verb, and a plural subject takes a plural verb.

Important Rules

  • He, She, It + singular verb: He plays football.
  • I, You, We, They + plural form of verb: They play football.
  • Two subjects joined by AND usually take a plural verb: Ravi and Arun are friends.
  • Each and Every take a singular verb: Each student is ready.
  • Everyone and Someone take singular verbs: Everyone is happy.
  • Neither...nor / Either...or: The verb generally agrees with the nearer subject.
  • Uncountable nouns take singular verbs: Water is important.
  • A collective noun can take a singular verb when treated as one group: The team is ready.

Correct the Sentences

IncorrectCorrect
He go to school.He goes to school.
They is happy.They are happy.
She have a pen.She has a pen.
Each students are ready.Each student is ready.
The boys plays.The boys play.
GRAMMAR 03

Degrees of Comparison

Grammar

Meaning

Degrees of comparison are used to compare people, animals, places or things.

Three Degrees

  • Positive: Describes one person or thing. Example: Ravi is tall.
  • Comparative: Compares two people or things. Example: Ravi is taller than Arun.
  • Superlative: Compares more than two. Example: Ravi is the tallest boy.

Important Examples

PositiveComparativeSuperlative
TallTallerTallest
SmallSmallerSmallest
BigBiggerBiggest
BeautifulMore beautifulMost beautiful
GoodBetterBest
BadWorseWorst
LittleLessLeast
Many / MuchMoreMost

Remember

  • Comparative degree usually uses THAN.
  • Superlative degree usually uses THE.
  • Do not use double comparison: more better is incorrect. Use better.
