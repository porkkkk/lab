2. Unit I – Poems

Lesson 1: The Pulley

Poet: George Herbert
PoemReligious poetryMetaphysical poetry

Simple explanation

God creates human beings and wants to give them many blessings. He gives them strength, beauty, wisdom, honour and pleasure. But He keeps one blessing, called rest, for Himself.

God thinks that if humans receive everything, they may become fully satisfied with worldly life. They may forget God and love the gifts more than the giver.

So God keeps rest away from humans. People may enjoy many things, but they still feel something is missing. This feeling makes them remember God and return to Him.

Story in easy points

  1. God prepares a glass full of blessings.
  2. He gives strength to humans.
  3. He gives beauty and wisdom.
  4. He gives honour and pleasure.
  5. Only rest remains in the glass.
  6. God decides to keep rest.
  7. He thinks humans may forget Him if they have everything.
  8. Human beings become restless.
  9. Their tiredness brings them closer to God.

Important themes

  • God's love and wisdom.
  • Human restlessness.
  • Faith and spirituality.
  • Worldly happiness is not complete happiness.
  • Humans need God.

Important symbols

SymbolMeaning
Glass of blessingsGod's gifts to humans.
StrengthPhysical power.
BeautyGood appearance.
WisdomKnowledge and understanding.
HonourRespect.
PleasureHappiness.
RestComplete satisfaction.
PulleyBringing humans closer to God.

Important literary devices

  • Metaphysical conceit: An unusual comparison between a pulley and the spiritual life of humans.
  • Symbolism: The glass represents God's blessings.
  • Religious imagery: God and His gifts are central to the poem.
Easy memory: God gives five blessings but keeps rest. Human restlessness makes them remember God.

Important 5-mark / 8-mark questions

1. What blessings does God give to man?
2. Why does God withhold rest?
3. Explain the pulley metaphor.
4. What is the central theme of the poem?
5. Explain the significance of the glass of blessings.
6. How does the poem show God's wisdom?
7. Explain human restlessness and spiritual dependence.
8. What is the moral lesson of the poem?

Lesson 2: Sonnet 30

Poet: William Shakespeare
PoemShakespearean Sonnet

Simple explanation

The speaker sits quietly and remembers his past. He thinks about lost opportunities, old problems and friends who have died. These memories make him very sad.

He feels that all his old sorrows are coming back again. But at the end, he remembers his dear friend. Suddenly, his sadness disappears. Thinking about his friend gives him happiness and comfort.

Poem in easy points

  1. The speaker remembers his past quietly.
  2. He thinks about things he has lost.
  3. He remembers his old friends.
  4. He feels sad about friends who have died.
  5. His old sorrows return to his mind.
  6. He feels deep emotional pain.
  7. He remembers his dear friend.
  8. His sadness changes into happiness.

Important themes

  • Memory and the past.
  • Friendship and love.
  • Death and loss.
  • Sadness and regret.
  • The healing power of friendship.
  • Passage of time.

Poem structure

ItemAnswer
Total lines14
StructureThree quatrains and one couplet
Rhyme schemeABAB CDCD EFEF GG
Poetic formShakespearean sonnet
MetreIambic pentameter

Literary devices

  • Metaphor: Memories are presented like a legal court session.
  • Imagery: Pictures of sadness, loss and death.
  • Alliteration: Repetition of similar starting sounds.
  • Financial imagery: The speaker describes emotional losses using words connected with money and accounts.
  • Volta: The change in mood in the final couplet.
Easy memory: Past memories bring sadness, but remembering a dear friend removes the sadness.

Important questions

1. Explain the meaning of "sessions of sweet silent thought".
2. Why does the speaker remember his past?
3. What does "death's dateless night" mean?
4. Explain the financial imagery.
5. What is the importance of friendship?
6. Explain the final two lines.
7. What are the main themes of Sonnet 30?
8. Explain the structure of the sonnet.

EssayHumour

Simple explanation

People wait for Sunday because it is a holiday. They plan to complete many activities, repair things, relax and enjoy their free time.

But unexpected problems and distractions come. They cannot finish their plans. Finally, they postpone the work to the next Sunday.

The same thing happens again and again. The writer uses humour to show how people waste time and postpone their work.

Important themes

  • Human nature.
  • Time management.
  • Household responsibilities.
  • Humour and irony.

Literary techniques

  • Humour: Makes readers laugh.
  • Irony: People plan work but do not complete it.

Lesson 6: The Last Leaf

Writer: O. Henry | Real name: William Sydney Porter
Short storyHope and sacrifice

Simple story

Sue and Johnsy are two young artists who live together. Johnsy becomes seriously ill with pneumonia. She looks outside the window and sees leaves falling from an ivy vine.

She believes that she will die when the last leaf falls. Sue tries to encourage her, but Johnsy loses hope.

Old Behrman is an elderly painter. He learns about Johnsy's belief. During a terrible storm, he goes outside and paints a leaf on the wall. It looks like a real leaf.

In the morning, Johnsy sees that the leaf is still there. She gets hope and decides to live. Slowly, she recovers. But Behrman catches pneumonia and dies. His painted leaf becomes his masterpiece.

7. Grammar – Complete Revision

A. Clauses and Sentences

What is a sentence?

A sentence is a group of words that gives complete meaning.

  • She is reading.
  • He plays football.
  • They are going to college.

What is a clause?

A clause is a group of words that has a subject and a verb.

  • She smiled.
  • Because he was late.
  • When the rain stopped.

Sentence and clause difference

SentenceClause
Gives complete meaning.May give complete or incomplete meaning.
Can stand alone.May depend on another clause.
Example: She is happy.Example: Because she is happy.

Types of clauses

1. Independent clause

Gives complete meaning.

Example: She is studying.

2. Dependent clause

Does not give complete meaning alone.

Example: Because he was sick.

3. Noun clause

Works like a noun.

Example: I know what you said.

4. Adjective clause

Describes a noun.

Example: The boy who won the prize is my friend.

5. Adverb clause

Explains time, reason or condition.

Example: Call me when you arrive.

Compound sentence

A compound sentence has two independent clauses joined together.

FANBOYS
F – For
A – And
N – Nor
B – But
O – Or
Y – Yet
S – So

Examples:

  • I wanted to go, but it was raining.
  • She studied hard, so she passed.
  • He was tired, yet he continued working.

B. Subject–Verb Agreement (Concord)

Definition: The verb must agree with the subject in number and person.

Important rules

Rule 1: Singular subject

Use a singular verb.

He plays football. She writes a letter.

Rule 2: Plural subject

Use a plural verb.

They play football. Boys sing.

Rule 3: Subjects joined by AND

Usually take a plural verb.

Ram and Ravi are friends.

Exception: Bread and butter is my favourite breakfast.

Rule 4: Either/or and Neither/nor

The verb agrees with the nearest subject.

Neither the teacher nor the students are present.

Rule 5: Each and Every

Take singular verbs.

Each student has a book.

Rule 6: Mathematics and News

Take singular verbs.

Mathematics is difficult. The news is good.

Rule 7: A number of / The number of

A number of students are absent.

The number of students is increasing.

Rule 8: There is / There are

There is a book. There are many books.

Rule 9: Collective nouns

The team is playing. The group is ready.

Rule 10: Time, money and distance

When considered as one unit, use singular verbs.

Ten kilometres is a long distance.

Important error corrections

Mathematics are difficult.Mathematics is difficult.

C. Degrees of Comparison

Definition: Degrees of comparison are used to compare people, places or things.

Three types

Positive

Describes one person or thing.

Ram is tall.

Comparative

Compares two things.

Ram is taller than Ravi.

Superlative

Compares three or more.

Ram is the tallest boy.

Important comparison table

PositiveComparativeSuperlative
TallTallerTallest
SmallSmallerSmallest
BigBiggerBiggest
FastFasterFastest
HappyHappierHappiest
EasyEasierEasiest
BeautifulMore beautifulMost beautiful
DifficultMore difficultMost difficult
GoodBetterBest
BadWorseWorst
LittleLessLeast
ManyMoreMost

Important rules

  1. Short adjectives usually take -er and -est.
  2. Long adjectives usually use more and most.
  3. Words ending in consonant + y change y into i.
  4. Some words have irregular forms.
  5. Use THAN with comparative degree.
  6. Use THE with superlative degree.
  7. Do not use double comparison.

Common mistakes

  • Wrong: She is more taller than me.
  • Correct: She is taller than me.
  • Wrong: He is the most tallest boy.
  • Correct: He is the tallest boy.

Used before a consonant sound.
